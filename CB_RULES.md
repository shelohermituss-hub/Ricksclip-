## HARD RULE NO. 2: NOTHING GENERIC PASSES. EVERYTHING IS ULTRA DETAILED. (CB 2026-11-11: "Can we add rules hard rule. Nothing generic wont pass. Everything need to be ultra detailed")
This is a gate, not advice. A frame that fails it is not shown to CB and not rendered further.
- **Generic = fail.** A plain box, ball tree, flat one-colour surface, typed font, filler word, star in place of a word, empty prop, standing or floor-sit pose where the story needs a real action, or a set that could belong to any film.
- **Every object in frame is specific to THIS story** and built from real parts, down to the small things: a wallet has pockets, stitching, a photo and flies that fly out; a board carries a real blueprint; a cart carries the ride's name; a loop under construction shows the crane and the missing piece.
- **Scale is checked:** trees about a quarter of the hero structure, a vehicle small around its rider, characters as large in frame as Rick's.
- **Poses are specific:** seated means a chair-sit with knees forward, sleeping means slumped with a tilt; body moves come from the trained motion library.
- **The test before CB sees it:** put the frame beside Rick's and name five specific details in every main object. The five details of every prop and of the hero object are written FIRST, in `PROPS.md`, and CB OKs that list before the set build (11a step 1b, check 2). If you cannot, or if Rick's shows a kind of thing ours lacks, it fails. Fix it first.
Full detail list: section 3, "PREMIUM DETAIL IN EVERYTHING".

## 1. Script and voice
- [ ] Rick script pattern; ALWAYS open on action, never a summary or setup line. ("always start action")
- [ ] Told like a 10-year-old tells a friend; story lines are never cut for length.
- [ ] Voice = Energetic Storyteller, pace 1.0, a mood tag on EVERY line. ("set standard at 1.0", "it sound all too flat")
- [ ] THE FIRST TAKE IS ENERGETIC, AND A QUOTE SOUNDS LIKE ANOTHER PERSON (CB 2026-11-15, lightning spear take 1: "too flat. and when someone say something you need to make a different tone"; the same "too flat" note fell on goldrush take 1). The first voice text already uses two- or three-word energy tags on every clause ("[excited, energetic, fast]", "[dramatic, building up]") and exclamation marks; no bare [soft]. Every quoted line gets a character tone clearly away from the narrator (pitch, pace or attitude: "[panicked, high-pitched, shouting]", "[smug, slow, deep voice]"), still the one narrator voice. Check by numbers before CB hears it: pitch range per sentence against the last approved take, and each quote at least about 2 semitones or a clear pace step away from its neighbours.
- [ ] Captions on, Rick style: Arial Bold white, thin black outline, 1-2 words, 3.9 % of frame height, baseline 71.5 %, no pop.

## 2. Every picture (the mechanism)
- [ ] FRAME 0 IS THE HOOK: a character doing the strange act with the hero object readable. Never an object close-up, at the start or as the loop frame. ("start frame should be something noticeable, a hook")
- [ ] One idea per picture = the words being spoken. Doer + thing + result in the same frame. New phrase = new picture or a clear move.
- [ ] EXACT WORDS AND ONE ACTION ON A NAMED WORD, EVERY BEAT (CB 2026-11-17: "what you can learn fromhere to make our video better", then "yes can add all"; source: the "Miner Who Fed Ghosts" prompt, project files rules/miner_prompt_2026-11-17.md). Every beat of the picture plan quotes the narration word for word from `voice/words.json` (never a summary), with its start second, and names ONE action that lands on ONE named word of it (on "knock" two taps come from the dark; "Tap. Creak. Tap." = one action on each word). A beat whose words are paraphrased or whose action floats free of a word is not a plan row.
- [ ] ONE PLANNED STILLNESS PER FILM (CB 2026-11-17: "what you can learn fromhere to make our video better", then "yes can add all"; source: the "Miner Who Fed Ghosts" prompt, project files rules/miner_prompt_2026-11-17.md). Every shot has something moving: a character, a prop, the light, the air (dust, drips, smoke) or the camera. The film may have ONE planned stillness, where the story itself stops (a question, the beat after a disaster): everything freezes on purpose, marked STILLNESS in the plan with its reason. Any other shot where nothing moves is a fault. The stillness is the only exception to "the camera never stops" (6, 11h M00).
- [ ] SILENT TEST: the action is acted out, so the story reads with the sound off. ("you understand the work without having the narration tell anything")
- [ ] 2-4 elements, subject big, nothing in front of the subject or the hero object. No empty frames.
- [ ] DEPTH IN EVERY SHOT (CB 2026-11-17: "what you can learn fromhere to make our video better", then "yes can add all"; source: the "Miner Who Fed Ghosts" prompt, project files rules/miner_prompt_2026-11-17.md): three layers, each from this set: a REAL, RECOGNISABLE prop close to the lens (a candle, a lunch tin, a beam, a pick handle; soft in focus but you can tell what it is), the characters, and a world that goes on far behind them. Never a grey or blurry blob near the lens, never an empty back wall. The near prop never sits between the lens and a face (11i). The plan names the near prop and the far world per row.
- [ ] Literal sense: the picture must make sense for the line and the place. ("why standing sleeping that not make sense")
- [ ] Stay in the story's world: analogy scenes use the same period, place and cast. No modern set in a period story.
- [ ] Exaggerate the idea as a funny prop or scale, not as random effects.

## 3. World and look (Rick's 3D world, never a flat drawing)
- [ ] The WORLD is real 3D with real materials, light, shadow and reflection. Only the characters are flat 2D cards. A flat 2D drawing of the whole scene is wrong. ("oh no. what are you doing that is not like ricks")
- [ ] HOW THE WORLD IS MADE, never skipped (CB 2026-11-11: "make image in code first and we can compare"; corrected twice on 2026-11-09 for skipping steps). Since 2026-11-15/16 the coded three.js frame with the kit3d look pack IS the final picture (Blender taken out, 11a). CB'S 7 BUILD STEPS for every place (CB 2026-11-11: "1. shot plan, 2. then the build, 3. then check camera angle, 4. environment overhaul, 5 post procession, 6 shading, 7 check the frames send fix to builder"), as they run now:
  1. Shot plan: the PICTURE_PLAN rows plus Rick frames of the same shot kind, before any model. Before it, the ONE-LINE PICTURE TEST: what the viewer understands with the sound off; no sentence = no shot.
  2. Build: the hero object first, alone, with real parts (THE CODED FRAME HAS REAL METAL below); then the sets from the shared look pack (11c) and the story list (STORY-DRIVEN WORLD BUILDING); characters act in library moves with hands ending on the object.
  3. Camera check: lens, height and angle against the Rick frame of the same shot kind; subject big, hero object not covered, faces above the caption line. Fix the camera BEFORE adding detail.
  4. Environment overhaul: density (foreground, mid and far layers, story lettering, air life, Rick's density bar), then LIGHT as its own step: one key light, hard long floor shadows, the subject brighter than the background.
  5. Post processing: the grade, the last picture step (11c).
  6. Shading: kit PBR materials on metal, gold, glass and wet things; normal or bump plus colour variation on stone, wood, brick, bark (no flat painted pictures).
  7. Check the frames: the automatic frame checks, then the lead LOOKS beside the Rick frame and the look_locked target, and sends ONE batched fix list to a FRESH builder (11b T2). The shot is checked MOVING, not only as a still. A frame CB approves is saved to references/look_locked as the next target.
  A flat sketch or a block set is never shown as the look. The old Blender form of these steps (Stage 1 Python toon scene, Stage 2 Blender materials, 30+ prop kinds / 600+ objects): rules/parked/BLENDER_PARKED.md.

## 5. Movement and acting
- [ ] NEVER A STATIC KIT POSE (CB 2026-11-13 evening, bomber armor animatic: "rules never use static kit pose. we made a rule for movement yesterday night", "no natural body movement, see the fireworks test we did it much better. you forgot something"). The kit's plain poses (stand, hold_out, knock, hold_up, hands_hips, grip_* used as a frozen stance ...) are blocking stand-ins only and never reach a storyboard, an animatic or CB. Every act of every character is a MOVE that plays through time (`m:<id>`): an episode move written for that action, or a trained library move for walks, climbs, kneels and carries (picked by action, feeling, energy and character, with its reason in the shot table), with the way in and the way out acted (walk in, kneel, climb, carry, turn). A prop grip is a library move with the grip on it, never a frozen grip pose. The bomber armor film went to CB with 100 of 103 poses static and not one walk: that is the failure this rule names. GATE (to be built into tools/film_plan.py; until then the lead counts by hand before any storyboard render): fail on any act whose pose is not `m:`, on a frozen move (`@=`), on `walk: true` with the planner's stand-in walk, on a film without `"tween": { "ones": true }`, and on `face_frames` above 0 (faces change as a step). A move the library lacks is designed in three styles and added first.
  CORRECTION (CB 2026-11-13, same evening: "the fireworks test clip. it move from itself. we didnt train it, followed another rule that we made"): natural body movement comes from TWO rules together, and the fireworks test used the second. (1) Trained library moves for the cycles and set pieces (walk, run, climb, kneel, carry ...). (2) STUDIO MOTION for every other action (`cloud_rules/motion/STUDIO_MOTION.md`): the body moves by itself through the action sequence NOTICE, READ, ANTICIPATE, HOLD, STRIKE, IMPACT, REACT, SETTLE, DEAD STILL, on ones, with the snap curve (about 70 % of the move in 5 frames, then a slow settle), the head leading a notice and lagging a strike, hands travelling on arcs, a counter-move before every strike, props and hanging things settling after the hand stops, all solved with BODY MECHANICS (fixed limb lengths, the hand goes to its target and the body follows). A pose change that jumps or blends straight from A to B without that sequence is a static kit pose in disguise and fails the same gate.

- [ ] CHARACTER ACTING, MICRO-EXPRESSIONS, PERSONALITY-DRIVEN PHYSICAL COMEDY (CB 2026-11-13 02:03 Bangkok, pasted as a rule; his wording: rules/CB_WORDING_ARCHIVE.md): characters think, feel, anticipate and react, never perform a mechanical action. - [ ] UNIVERSAL VISUAL COMEDY AND GAG SYSTEM (CB 2026-11-13 02:05 Bangkok, pasted as a rule; his text in full: rules/CB_WORDING_ARCHIVE.md): find gag chances in every scene without waiting for the script; invent original jokes from the character's goal, personality, actions, props, place and situation. Mechanisms: situational comedy, visual metaphor, prop comedy, character-driven humour, expectation reversal, escalation, physical comedy (believable body mechanics), background comedy that never pulls focus, silent storytelling, comedic timing (setup, anticipation, pause, reveal, reaction, recovery; a short stillness sells the punchline). Never repeat a joke template; a different mechanism per scene; one strong readable gag beats several weak ones; never interrupt key narration. Each gag has setup, anticipation, reveal, reaction and exit, sold with face, body, props, secondary motion and framing, with contact and grounding kept. ## 10. THE PROVEN BUILD METHOD (pin stretch test, CB 2026-11-14: "Yes, rebuild film". Proof: episodes/armor-no-holes/stretches/armor-pins-test)
- A film is built in STRETCHES of one place each (10-25 s), one builder per stretch for the build, and a FRESH builder for every fix round who starts from the stretch's STATE.md (section 11, T2; changed 2026-11-15 because a builder kept for 13 hours cost 184 million tokens). Each stretch is ONE continuous take: the camera flies, lands, rests, flies again; no cut inside a stretch. Stretches join only where the place changes, through an object or a 8-12 frame blend.
- Arms (CB 2026-11-16; section 4): one slight smooth curve, never a triangle or corner; arms a set draws itself use the shared soft_arm.js piece, never two cylinders with an elbow; hands only a plain black circle. Zoom every arm before reporting.
- Every act of every character is an episode move written for that act (a moves file in the stretch's cast folder, loaded as `m:../../<file>/<style>`), drawn new on every frame: `tween.ones` true, `face_frames` 0. Count before any render: plain kit poses 0, frozen moves 0.
- Things that go in, on or at something FLY there and HIT: flight with a streak, a one-frame hit, a mark, a quiver, dust, and the thing that is hit shudders. Nothing just appears.
- Act frames are plain frame numbers taken from words.json, never shifted by a tool.
- Sheets made for every render and looked at by the builder AND the lead before CB: a strip at 4 frames per second, the LINE CHECK (each line's words beside its frames: do I see what it says?), feet and eyes zooms, a phone-size sheet.
- Still owed after the test, check on every stretch: nobody cut by the frame edge at any frame of a rest; floor at most a third of a wide; the small story thing (a pin, a chip, a chalk ring) large enough to read on a phone at the moment its word is said; props in hands as real 3D props where the kit can.
- Renders: full-size frames in parallel chunks (`episodes/armor-no-holes/stretches/armor-pins-test/render_final.sh` is the pattern). With several builders on one PC, at most 3 render processes per builder.
- CLOSE-UPS ON WHAT THE VOICE NAMES, AS A SECONDARY RULE THAT MUST MATCH THE SCENE (CB 2026-11-14 on the joined armor film: "there are some like close up missing and frame by narration", "When you say hole, you need to show a hole", "Zoom to a hole"; then, correcting the lead who made it the top rule: "It's not primary, it's secondary. It needs to match the scene."). The scene comes first: its staging, its acting, the flow of the take. Where it fits that scene, the camera flies in and lands close on the thing the voice names, on the word, rests a beat and flies on; a named part may be highlighted with an effect. Never force a close-up on every noun and never chop a take into a string of inserts.
- NOBODY CUT IN HALF, STANCES PHYSICALLY RIGHT (CB same day: "at second 9, he had removed by half", "when he stands on the ladder, it's like a wrong position"). Check every frame of a rest for a figure cut by the frame edge; anyone on a ladder, box or step has both soles on a visible tread and the body on the right side of it. Zoom on it before sending.
- HOW RICK DOES IT, MEASURED (4 films, 106 named things; study `episodes/armor-no-holes/refs/closeup_study/CLOSEUP_STUDY.md`, 2026-11-14): 96 % of named things are shown on their word, about 40 % close, 57 % in the scene view. A close-up is a push inside the running take (0.3-0.5 s in), the thing DOES something for about 1 s, then the camera pulls back or tilts to a face; a hand, tool or face is in it, light and place match the wide, the action never stops. A part is highlighted with the part itself (glow or colour running along it, glint, dust, a real marker); no outlines, arrows or floating labels, and the rest is not dimmed without a real cause. "Many of one thing" is revealed in under half a second with a hand placing the newest.
- RENDER ON THE GRAPHICS CARD (CB 2026-11-14: "Can you not use gpu all work?"). `templates/threejs_film/render_gpu.mjs` draws the three.js frames on the RTX 2070 SUPER: about 0.55-0.9 s per full-size frame against 4.7 s in software, picture equal apart from antialiased edges (tested on one film; `GPU_RENDER.md`). One process normally, two only when the card is free, never three, never while Blender uses the card. Never mix software and GPU frames inside one stretch. Check one frame per place both ways before a full run. Why the first try was black: a `pow()` of a slightly negative number in a set shader gives not-a-number on the card, and the bloom blur spread it; any new set shader must guard `pow`, `sqrt` and divisions.

2b. THE CAMERA PLAN, BEFORE ANY STRETCH RENDERS IN MOTION (CB 2026-11-16 after elevator, Monopoly, braking lines and fake surgery all came out with a fast, jumpy camera: "previousd work didnt have this issue now the issue is back. you need to know before this happen. so avoid it"; "you can not make one mesurement as norm since every episode is different"). Why it came back: the old stage 5 (camera-director + `film_plan.py check`) had no place in this lean list, so the stretch builders put one camera station on every picture-plan row (about 80 in 74 s), and "one flying take" (11f) turned the cuts between them into whips. The films before the lean pipeline (lightning spear, goldrush) had the camera step and did not have the fault. Rule:
   - A PICTURE-PLAN ROW IS NOT A CAMERA STATION. Rows say what the viewer must see on a word; one camera move flows through several rows (follow the thing that moves, push in, rise up the tall thing, cross the set).
   - The `camera-director` writes each stretch's camera from the built sets BEFORE its builder renders motion: one flowing idea per place, with the reason for every move from this scene, written in the stretch's CAMERA_NOTE.md.
   - The scene decides the numbers, not one norm: `python tools/film_plan.py check` runs in every stretch's run.sh before any render and prints the camera bands as a WARNING list. A camera band may be outside only with this scene's reason in CAMERA_NOTE.md. A station teleport (a jump in one frame inside a take) and an unplanned whip are always FAIL.
   - CAMERA V2 ON EVERY NEW FILM (CB 2026-11-16 "Yes, fix and re-test"; proof reel episodes/_tests/camera_rules_2026-11-16/CAMERA_TEST_V2.md, CINEMATOGRAPHY.md section 7). THE EFFECT IS NOT A FIXED LIST (CB 2026-11-16: "for power moments dont fix it. it can be anything that match teh scenen"): invent whatever the scene and its objects call for. Rick's examples (shockwave ring, light-ray burst, crack veins, aura, spiral eyes, spark trail, explosion, smoke trails) and the pieces in templates/pieces/fx/power/ are a starting point and a time saver, never a menu to pick from. The picture stays calm and real between them; the effect lands because it breaks the calm. The picture plan names each power moment (word, effect, why in this story). Until the power-effects pieces exist in templates/pieces/fx/, the stretch builder builds the effect inside its moving take (11a: never as a round on still frames).
- DIRECT POWER MOMENTS LIKE A MANGA OR ANIME VISUAL DIRECTOR (CB 2026-11-16: "think like a manga or anime visual director for suoper power"). A power moment is a short directed sequence, not a single effect laid over the shot. The anime toolbox, used only where the scene earns it:
  - TIMING. An anticipation hold of 6-12 frames (the world goes quiet, the character's eyes narrow, the camera creeps in). Then the IMPACT FRAME: 2-4 frames of a high-contrast smash, which can be a colour-inverted or two-tone flash, the background dropped to flat colour or speed lines, and the silhouette in ink. Then a held peak of 0.5-1 s, then the settle. The effect is posed on the beat, not smoothly faded.
  - FRAMING. A snap push-in or crash zoom on the impact. A freeze frame with a slow push on the peak. A low hero angle for strength. An extreme close-up on the eyes before the act.
  - DRAWN LINES. Focus lines (radial lines closing in on the face or the object), speed lines behind fast moves, smear frames on fast swings, impact stars and shock lines at the contact point.
  - BACKGROUND. On the emotional peak the background can drop away to an abstract field (flat colour, light streaks, a sparkle or flame pattern) and come back on the settle.
  - SMALL MARKS. A sweat drop, an anger vein mark, a sparkle on a smug grin, a glint on glasses, steam from the ears, and an aura that flickers on 2s.
  - CAMERA AND SOUND. A camera shake on the hit (3-8 frames, decaying), always paired with a sound hit on the same frame.
  - THE SCENE DECIDES. These are tools, not a checklist. Choose what this scene's character and objects call for: the tower guard's laugh can blast a shockwave of hotel tokens; the owner's vanity can turn the background into a sparkle field around his tie. Kid-safe, never gory. Outside the power moments the film stays calm and real, so they hit.
- IMPOSSIBLE VIEWS (CB 2026-11-16: "there is also one rule impossible view", then "Yes, add it"). One or two times per film, on a strong story moment, the camera sits where no real camera could be.
  - Rick's examples: inside the sheriff's eye, coming out as the driver's view; inside the old TV, looking out into the pink cell; straight down into the paint bucket as the red pours in; between the drilled holes of the cart board as the tubes slide in.
  - It must match the story: it shows the moment from the thing that matters most, the object, the body part or the hidden place.
  - Examples for our films:
    - from inside the Monopoly board, looking up as it is peeled open, with the silk unfolding over the lens;
    - from behind the mirror glass, looking out at the lady fixing her hair;
    - from inside the stuffed complaint box as the lid lifts;
    - from road level, as the stripes rush over the lens.
  - Enter and leave it with a camera move (a fly-through, a push or a dissolve on motion), never a hard cut. The frame edges show where we are (the inside of the board, the mirror's back, the box walls), so it reads at once.
  - The picture plan marks the row "IMPOSSIBLE VIEW", with where the camera is and why. Kid-safe: inside a body only in a cartoon way, never gory.
- THE LAST FRAME IS OFTEN THE HERO CALM AND BIG IN FRONT OF THE HUGE RESULT (the cart film ends on a mushroom cloud behind the calm inventor).
- WORDS IN THE WORLD: the key term and one key number appear on a sign or prop on their word ("TARGET FIXATION" on a green highway sign; "DAYS SINCE LAST CRASH 0"; the paint sheet "P-618 BAKER-MILLER PINK"). Exaggerated props carry the joke (a giant flashing neon arrow on the tree; a knitted scarf as long as the cell).

### 11g. THE WORD MADE EXTREME: the universal visual gag (CB 2026-11-16, on two frames of Rick's "Why Drivers Started Filming The Road": "is there a rule gag. like everything need to be extrme. sample he get nice fat check and the visual make a mountain of money. is a gag and visual funny. that can be use universal for any matching scene")
- WHAT RICK DOES. When the voice says an amount, a size, a speed, a feeling or a figure of speech, the picture takes it LITERALLY and pushes it far past real, in the scene's own world, on that word. "A nice fat check" becomes a mountain of banknotes pouring over the whole front lawn while the fraudster grins beside his red car. "All that pain he's in" becomes him grinning in a hospital bed parked ON his own lawn, head bandaged, while the insurance man hands over a case of money. More from his films: "300 lb of tattoos" is a wall of a man; "[line from a reference film left out]" shows the milk cart; "Barbie's going to love it" (the pink cell); the giant flashing neon "HIT THIS TREE" arrow.
- HOW TO FIND THEM. When the picture plan is written, the writer marks every word of these kinds:
  - AMOUNT ("fat check", "hundreds", "a fortune", "every week", "stuffed");
  - SIZE OR STRENGTH ("huge", "300 lb", "tiny");
  - SPEED OR TIME ("so slow", "so fast", "all night", "years");
  - FEELING ("furious", "terrified", "in pain");
  - an IDIOM or comparison ("slower than the stairs", "like a sandwich").
  For each, ask: what would it look like if it were literally, absurdly true right here? Then pick the best.
- THE MEASURE. About 4-8 extreme gags in a 60-75 s film, at most ONE per line, and never stacked on the same beat (section 9: one action or one feeling per beat).
  - Each one is pushed 10-100 times past real: a mountain, not a pile; a bed on the lawn, not a bandage.
  - It is built from the story's own objects and place, never a stock effect.
  - It is timed so the picture lands ON the word and then holds about 1 s, so it reads.
- KEEP THE STORY TRUE. The gag exaggerates the PICTURE, never the fact: the voice keeps the checked number. A mountain of money for "a fat check" is fine; a caption saying a wrong sum is not.
- IN THE PLAN. The PICTURE_PLAN "effect or gag" column marks these rows "EXTREME:" plus the picture, e.g. EXTREME: complaint cards stacked to the ceiling, the lid riding on top. The count goes in the end count with the power moments (11f).

### 11h. CAMERA MOVE LIBRARY, PICKED BY STORY JOB (CB 2026-11-16, decision card "Write the rules now", with "show me all camera moves that ricks clips use in all his video. and create a template with explanation like this. so we can copy all camera moves"). Source: references/camera_moves/RICK_CAMERA_MOVES_LIBRARY.md + camera_moves_template.json (27 moves M00-M26, measured on all 26 Rick films; frames private).
1. Every picture-plan row carries a story-job tag: HOOK, SETUP, TALK, ACT, EXPLAIN, ANALOGY, TURN, PLACE, CLIMAX, END.
2. The camera-director picks the move from the library menu for that tag and writes "row -> Mxx, attached to (thing, line or object), reason" in CAMERA_NOTE.md. Rows with the same tag in one place share ONE move. No move without a reason; an orbit (M16) only at a story peak.
3. Copy the move's stations from camera_moves_template.json and move them onto the set's anchors; keep the shape (distances, heights, travel, hold).
4. Targets per film (Rick, 26 films): flying 31-51 %, rest 30-45 %, median flight 0.9-1.2 s, at least 2 flights of 3 s or longer, each named by what it follows; first flight by 1 s; the CLIMAX holds the film's longest flight (M14); EXPLAIN and ANALOGY each sit inside one travel (M07+M06, M08); the END is a pull back (M26); pull backs about one in four flights, each a reveal.
5. Camera-safe inside every move: character height within 10 degrees down when a face is in frame, never pass behind a figure, views from apply_looks; run frame_checks and validate.mjs at 0/25/50/75/100 % of each flight, not only on rests.
6. Tool gaps until film_plan.py is updated: follow works for characters only (props, effects and set lines need pass-points every 0.5 s); curves need pass-point stations (hold 0); travel over 3 s must be set by hand; the gate "word stations not at rest on their word" is to be removed (it forces a stop per noun).
7. THE ATTENTION CHAIN (CB 2026-11-16, his 12 camera-director points: "Make the camera follow the audience's attention: face -> hand -> prop -> consequence -> reaction"). Every ACT, CLIMAX and TURN row is planned as this chain: the face that decides, the hand that acts, the prop it acts on, what happens because of it, and the face that reacts. One flight with 3-5 short landings (M10), not five framings. CAMERA_NOTE.md lists the five anchors for each such row; a link may be skipped only with a reason.
8. EVERY STATION IS A STORY KEYFRAME (points 2, 3 and 8). Its purpose is one of: reveal, track, inspect, compare, follow the action, show the reaction. It sits on a narration word or an action frame, and it states position, target (aim), framing, lens, focus target and easing. The focus target is the thing the station is about (a face, a hand, a prop) and drives the world depth of field; until film_plan.py has a focus key, CAMERA_NOTE.md names it. Positions come from the one world (11d2): the camera aims at where the character or prop really is on that frame.
9. EVERY MOVE IS CHECKED MOVING (points 9 and 10). At 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 % of each flight: nothing blocks the subject (occlusion), each silhouette reads, the camera does not pass through a wall or a figure (collision), faces and the hero prop are inside the safe frame and clear of the caption band, focus is on the focus target, the hero prop is visible. Camera direction is approved only from a moving preview (11a gate 2, section 6), never from stills alone.
10. CAMERA DON'TS: WHAT MUST NEVER HAPPEN (CB 2026-11-16: "should we have what should not happend? also added to it"; from his points 7, 11 and 12 and from his past rejections). Any one of these in a version = not ready for CB.
   - A character teleports or morphs to a new place or pose; places change only by a seen move or a real cut (section 5 NO TELEPORT, NO MORPHING).
   - The camera turns, moves, squashes or re-poses a character, or changes an approved look, size, colour or face. The camera only looks at the one world (11d2, camsafe, point 12).
   - An edge-on or thin card, a flattened face, a stretched or distorted body, the wrong view for the angle, a back view of the subject, an eyeline that misses its target, feet off the ground, a hand that does not touch its prop (camsafe validate.mjs, frame_checks seat, slide and prop_in_hand).
   - A cut or a new framing for every sentence or every noun; a hard cut inside one place; more than 1 hard cut per 10 s (6b, 11f).
   - A camera that hops in short 0.3-0.5 s moves between many stations, or stops dead and freezes; a rest is always alive (M00).
   - A decorative orbit, a random zoom or push, a random shake, a move with no story job; shake only on a hit, 3-8 frames, decaying, with its sound (11f).
   - A jerky, whipped or linear move: no ease, a sudden direction flip, more than about 40 px per frame (60 only for the one planned crash zoom) (11f).
   - Tilt (roll) while resting; roll only inside a move and level on landing.
   - A long slow fade, or a transition with no motion; place changes are a flight, a dissolve on motion, a match or a fly-through (6, 6b, 11f).
   - A wide where a close-up must explain an object or an action; the subject blocked, cropped by accident, under the caption band, or lost in empty space (section 6).
   - The camera passing behind a figure, or below 10 degrees down on a face at character height when it is close (camsafe FACE_BEHIND, STEEP, LOW).
   - The screen direction, object positions or blocking flipping between shots of the same moment (11d2).
   - Camera direction judged or approved from stills alone.
