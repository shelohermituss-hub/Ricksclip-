# PICTURE PLAN: what the viewer sees on each word (format, CB_RULES section 11d, CB 2026-11-15)

This is a FORMAT, not content. Every film gets its own plan, written fresh from its own script. No row is ever copied from another film.
CB approves it together with the rough board (the one gate). It is text: a change here costs seconds; the same change on a built film costs hours.
Why it exists: on the armor film about 14 of CB's 23 notes were "the thing the voice names is not shown, not close enough or not highlighted" ("When you say hole, you need to show a hole", "Chalk the circle"). Every one of them could have been decided on paper.

## How to write it
1. Read the script line by line with `words.json` open. For every thing or action the voice NAMES, write one row.
2. Decide per row how it is shown, and write WHY for THIS story:
   - CLOSE: the camera pushes in (0.3-0.5 s), the thing does something for about a second, the camera pulls back to a face.
   - IN SCENE: it is clearly in the frame on its word, at the scene's own framing.
   - NOT SHOWN: only when showing it would break the scene; say why.
   "The voice says it" is not a reason. A reason is what the thing does in the story: it is the twist, the proof, the joke, the thing the hero touches, the number the viewer must believe.
3. The story decides, not a quota. CB 2026-11-14: "It's not primary, it's secondary. It needs to match the scene." A close-up goes in only where it fits the staging, the acting and the flow of the take. Rick's measured numbers (96 % of named things shown on their word, about 40 % of them close) are a check on the TOTAL at the end, never a target per line.
4. Feeling: what the character in frame feels on that word, in plain words from this story ("smug: he has heard this joke before"), not a list word like "happy".
5. Effect or gag: only one that grows out of THIS story's world and objects (flies out of this mess-hall trash barrel; pins that hit the drawing like bullets). Leave it empty rather than add a stock effect.
6. Mark the HOOK row (frame 0), the TWIST row, the ONE SIGNATURE row of the film (step 11), and the LAST FRAME row.
7. A piece from the library (`templates/pieces/INDEX.md`, 17 pieces from the armor film) is a technique: how a push-in lands, how a highlight glows, how a hop is timed. Name the piece AND what changes for this story (which object, which light, which character). A piece used unchanged is generic and fails Hard Rule No. 2.

8. EXTREME GAGS (CB_RULES 11g, CB 2026-11-16). Mark every word of amount, size, speed, feeling or figure of speech, and for the best 4-8 write "EXTREME: <the word taken literally, 10-100 times past real, built from this story's objects>" in the effect or gag column. Rick: "a nice fat check" becomes a mountain of banknotes over the whole lawn. At most one per line; the voice keeps the true number.
9. POWER MOMENTS AND POV (CB_RULES 11f). Name the 3-5 drawn power moments (word, effect, why). The effect is whatever matches THIS scene, not a pick from a list (CB 2026-11-16). Mark POV rows with what fills the frame edges and how the camera enters and leaves (through an eye or an object). Put the key term or number on a sign in the world. Mark 1-2 rows "IMPOSSIBLE VIEW" (the camera where no real camera could be, inside the object or the hidden place that matters most), with where the camera is, why, and how we fly in and out.

10. STORY JOB (CB_RULES 11h): tag every row HOOK, SETUP, TALK, ACT, EXPLAIN, ANALOGY, TURN, PLACE, CLIMAX or END; the camera-director picks the move for that job.
11. THE CREATIVE EDGE (CB_RULES 12, CB 2026-11-16 "ok add it"): read `CREATIVE.md` (written by the creative-director before this plan is final). Put ONE acted gag in every beat's "acted gag" column (who, the gag, its mechanism, the faces or move it needs; no mechanism twice in the film; readable with the sound off), mark the ONE row "SIGNATURE" (the picture Rick would never make, around the hero object, on the TURN or CLIMAX), and write the colour world into LOOK_BIBLE.md. An idea from CREATIVE.md that does not fit the scene is left out and the plan says why in one line.

13. THE MINER LESSONS (CB_RULES sections 2, 3, 11i, 12c; CB 2026-11-17 "yes can add all"). In every row: the EXACT words from words.json (never a summary) and ONE action on ONE named word; the LIGHT SOURCE you see and how the light has changed since the last beat; the NEAR-LENS PROP (real, recognisable, from this set) and the FAR WORLD behind. Mark the ONE row "STILLNESS" (if the film has one; every other row has motion) and every "FLASHBACK" or "DREAM" row (the only rows that may get a vignette). The hero's signature colour is worn by nobody else; the props named here are in PROPS.md per set, with their wear.

12. A ROW IS NOT A CAMERA STATION (CB_RULES 11a 2b). The rows say what must be seen on each word. The camera-director then plans a few flowing moves per stretch that pass through several rows; never one camera stop per row.

## After CB approves
- Every row is a must for the builder of that stretch. The line-check sheet is ordered by these rows: one strip per row, the frames at its word.
- A fix note from CB on a film is first checked against the plan: was the row there? If not, the plan gets the row, so the next film's writer sees the kind of thing that was missed.
- At the end count: named things shown on their word, and how many close. Far under Rick's numbers means the plan was thin; far over means a noun checklist. Say which in the report.
