## What to take (each with how we apply it)
1. **Never a pure explainer. Always a person doing a strange thing.**
   - Why: the two clips with no hero and no mocker ("how do painkillers work", "the slot machine…") got 0.1-0.3 M views; every story with a hero got millions.
   - How: the writer's check fails any script whose frame 0 is not a person acting. All our current scripts pass.
2. **The biggest hits end on a physical disaster or rescue that proves the mocked person right:** the palace in rubble, the rails kicking sideways under a train, the wave lifting the ship, the rival's cart at the bottom of the loop.
   - Why: the payoff is a PICTURE, not a fact.
   - How: the last line of every script must be an image you can read with the sound off.
   - To check on the waiting list: fake surgery ends on a statement ("the patients never missed it"). It needs a final image, e.g. the veteran running up the hospital stairs past the surgeon.
3. **Rival or mocker structure: the mocker's own action causes his fall.** In Roller Coasters the rival squeezes the loop to sabotage it and rides the death loop himself. In Railroads the rival's "perfect" rails buckle.
   - How: the twist should come FROM the mocker's own action where possible, not only land on him.
4. **Rick freely invents characters around a true mechanism, and even an origin-legend last line** ("[line from a reference film left out]"). The mechanism is real (insurance fraud, dash cams); the characters and their order are story.
   - How: this backs our "told as lore" approach (elevator) and allows invented heroes on top of a checked mechanism. We keep our fact check for every number and claim.
5. **"You see" can come twice:** once early as a one-line setup ("[line from a reference film left out] once late for the reveal (Roller Coasters).
   - How: allowed in our scripts; the late one stays at about 70-80 %.
6. **Length: his newest are 62-63 s (median 64).** Ours are longer: Monopoly 78 s, elevator 72 s, the waiting list 74-76 s now that the pauses are back.
   - How: make films shorter by writing FEWER WORDS (about 195-210 instead of 226-263), never by cutting audio (CB: "you trim too much").
7. **Titles that won:**
   - "The Ancient Jar That Warned an Empire", "Why Perfect Railroads Don't Work", "Why Sailors Carried Storm Oil". The pattern is "Why <ordinary thing> <surprising verb>" or "The <object> That <did something>", 4-7 words.
   - The weakest titles explain ("How Painkillers Actually Work", "The Science Behind Slot Machines").
   - How: YOUTUBE.md titles follow the winning pattern and never "How X works" or "The science behind".
8. **Sad or dark fiction did worst** (Memory 0.56 M; Button 3.8 M). Clever-trick and disaster-proof stories did best.
   - How: prefer clever trick or proof stories when picking ideas. Our waiting list fits.

## Proposed changes to the skill (need CB's OK before they go into CB_RULES)
- **Writer rule:** the last line is a picture (point 2), the twist comes from the mocker's own action where the story allows (point 3), 195-210 words (point 6), no pure explainers (point 1).
