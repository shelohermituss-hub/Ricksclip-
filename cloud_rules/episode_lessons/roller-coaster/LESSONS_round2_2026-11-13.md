# What I learned from CB's roller coaster notes (2026-11-12 18:50Z)

1. A crank is turned, not held. The handle must go round with the fists ON the grip every frame. Method: measure the two fists per frame in the storyboard, put the crank hub at their centre, and drive the crank angle from that measurement (plan/park_crank.json). A hand-made 8-pose loop alone slides off the grip.
2. Words on a banner never move. Banners are stretched painted canvas on wooden battens, no wind, no folds. Paint the letters INTO the cloth's own material; a see-through word plane in front gets cut by a close character card. Nothing hangs in front of the words (the work lamp was moved above the banner).
3. Mechanical motion follows the sound. The ratchet clicks three times, so the loop squeezes in three steps (24.20, 24.49, 24.74 s), each with a small overshoot, never one smooth bend. When a part changes shape, hide every other version of it (the old round loop showed through as "two roller coasters").
4. Drivers sit INSIDE the car: whole face in the windscreen, hat under the roof edge. Check the close shot of every vehicle with a person in it.
5. Highlights on metal travel. The glow at the top of the loop starts small and spreads outward along the rail, and its light slides along it; it never sits still.
6. An effect never freezes before the end. The last explosion keeps rising and spreading, the ring keeps opening and the bits keep flying out until the final frame.
7. Wood must read as wood: grain along each part, darker knots, warm brown, not a flat beige.
8. Camera (kept from the earlier measurements): one slow continuous move per beat, never a still frame, joins by the move or a short blend, pull back to reveal about one move in eight, half our usual speed.
9. Costume shadow side (CB rule, cart army version): hats, clothes and accessories get a darker side, the face never does.
