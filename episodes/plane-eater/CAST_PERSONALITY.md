# CAST PERSONALITY: The Man Who Ate An Airplane (writer, 2026-11-21; CB_RULES section 5, the 13 points)

Three people: Michel (hero), the Doctor, the Mechanic. No more extras (brief: at most 2).
Michel is a real man (FACTS): this is OUR OWN cartoon design from the locked cast style, not a likeness of any photo of him.
Bodies from the three-size bible; dressed from the locked outfit library (`dress(S, key, {...})`); short costumes, no long cloth.
Era: France, 1978-1980. Period check: wide collars, flares, waxed handlebar moustaches on stage performers, Breton stripes, chrome-and-bakelite X-ray box, push-button cash-register-style stamp.

## COLOUR RULES FOR PEOPLE (proposal to the lead for LOOK_BIBLE, 12c; the lead decides)
- HERO SIGNATURE COLOUR: TOMATO RED (about `#D8342A`), only on Michel: his bow tie and the red checks of his tucked-in gingham napkin. No other person, and no prop on a person, carries it.
  Why: (1) red reads as "dinner" (checked bistro tablecloth) at first sight; (2) it pops against the white-and-navy Cessna; (3) it is far from the cool cyan X-ray light, so he stays the focal point in the dark X-ray room; (4) the napkin triangle is his silhouette key and the red keeps it readable at 360 px.
- So: the Cessna is WHITE with NAVY stripes (no red stripe), the stage curtain is deep plum, the coffin is honey wood, no red bike, no red cart, no red TV.
- NAVY belongs to the plane stripes and Michel's shirt stripes (they rhyme: the man and his meal). Doctor and Mechanic wear no navy stripes.

Contrast comedy for the whole film: a calm, polite gourmet eating the impossible, beside two men who cannot believe it. The fine-dining manners never break, whatever is on the plate.

## MICHEL "MONSIEUR MANGETOUT" (hero; THIN body, about 28)
Look at first sight: a proud, polite stage showman at a fancy dinner. Breton shirt (navy and white stripes, boat neck), tomato-red bow tie, black high-waisted trousers with black braces, polished black shoes. Glossy black hair, combed back with a side part, one curl falling on the brow.
**Signature silhouette (fixed, every view): the big waxed handlebar moustache whose tips curl UP like two fork tines, over the white-and-red gingham napkin tucked into his collar (a triangle on his chest). Knife in the right fist, fork in the left, held up like a conductor's batons.**
1. Temperament: cheerful, unhurried, a perfect host to his own meal. Never greedy, always polite.
2. Confidence vs skill: total confidence, and real skill: he is the only person alive who can do this. He treats it as cuisine, not a stunt.
3. Fears: a sharp edge (so every bite is cut small and neat); being called a fake.
4. Motivation: the show and the record; to be the most famous eater in the world.
5. Emotional habits: pleasure goes into his moustache (the tips twitch up); doubt from others makes him more polite, never angry.
6. Quirks: taps metal with his fork and listens like a chef testing a melon; sniffs a part before cutting it; dabs both moustache tips with the napkin corner after every bite; pinky out on every glass.
7. Posture: upright and elegant at the table, chest a little forward, chin up, elbows OFF the table.
8. Movement rhythm: slow and precise for cutting and eating (a surgeon at dinner); quick bright flourishes for the showman moves (the bow, the wink).
9. Signature gestures (5): (a) knife and fork raised, tines up, like a conductor about to start (frame 0 and last frame: the loop); (b) the fork tap and the listen, head tilted; (c) the napkin dab on both moustache tips; (d) pinky-out sip from the glass of oil; (e) the slow wink to the lens with a moustache twitch.
10. Reaction speed: SLOW and small. When others panic he takes one more bite.
11. Relationships: to the Mechanic, a polite customer to a waiter (he never notices that the plane was the Mechanic's pride); to the Doctor, a proud patient posing for his portrait; to the plane, a chef to a fine dish.
12. Status: a strange man with cutlery in a hangar (line 1), an artist at work (2-4), a medical marvel (5), a champion (6-8), the smug winner over airline food (last frame). He does not change; the world catches up with him.
13. Speaks: only the THOUGHT "Lunch!" (4.02-4.80): his mouth does NOT open; his lips smack once and his tongue licks the left moustache tip on "Lunch". Everything else is narrator; his mouth moves only to chew.

## THE DOCTOR (extra 1; FAT body, short, about 60)
Look at first sight: a careful, serious old hospital doctor who has seen everything until today. Short white coat over a grey knitted waistcoat and a dark green tie, grey trousers, brown shoes. Bald dome with a white horseshoe of hair, bushy white brows.
**Signature detail (fixed): half-moon glasses low on the nose and a big X-ray film held up to the light in both hands.**
1. Temperament: calm, dry, scientific. 2. Confidence vs skill: both high, until the X-ray. 3. Fears: being fooled by a stage trick. 4. Motivation: proof. 5. Habits: shock goes to his glasses (they slide down to the nose tip). 6. Quirks: holds the X-ray at arm's length, then very close; taps it with a pen. 7. Posture: leaning in, belly first. 8. Rhythm: slow, then one sudden freeze. 9. Gestures (5): (a) the X-ray held up to the lightbox; (b) the pen tap on the film; (c) glasses pushed up, they slide down again; (d) the slow head shake; (e) the official stamp slammed with both hands (line 8). 10. Reaction speed: a slow double take (look, look away, LOOK). 11. Relationships: to Michel, a sceptic who becomes his proof. 12. Status: the judge who becomes the witness. 13. Never speaks.

## THE MECHANIC (extra 2, the mocking onlooker; STANDARD body, about 45)
Look at first sight: a proud, oily airfield mechanic who loves this little plane. Olive-green overalls with the sleeves rolled, a grey cap with a little wing badge, an oily rag in the back pocket, a big spanner. Stubble, a pencil behind the ear.
**Signature detail (fixed): the oily rag he wipes the plane with and the spanner that drops whenever he is shocked.**
1. Temperament: jokey, loud, proud of his machine. 2. Confidence vs skill: a good mechanic, sure that nobody eats a plane. 3. Fears: losing his plane (he does, bite by bite). 4. Motivation: to keep the Cessna whole. 5. Habits: laughs first, panics second. 6. Quirks: polishes the propeller while talking; counts parts on his fingers. 7. Posture: hands on hips, belly out, then shoulders up to the ears. 8. Rhythm: big and quick. 9. Gestures (5): (a) the laugh with a slap on his knee (line 1); (b) the spanner drop (line 2); (c) hugging the tire to save it (line 4); (d) the defeated wheel-in of the dessert trolley (line 7); (e) the polite waiter's offer of the airline tray (line 8). 10. Reaction speed: INSTANT and big (the opposite of Michel). 11. Relationships: to Michel, a mocker who becomes his waiter. 12. Status: the boss of the hangar (line 1), the loser of his plane (2-6), the hero's servant (7-8). 13. Never speaks.
