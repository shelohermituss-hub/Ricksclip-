# apron_STORY.md: THE AIRFIELD APRON, AERODROME DE VILLEBRUNE (set-builder 2026-11-21)
Invented aero-club field in the French countryside, 1978. Rows 1-18, 24-34 (PICTURE_PLAN v2). Map: X east, Y north; Cessna spot = origin, nose south.

## Required location
Small grass airfield at noon: one pastel hangar (pale sage corrugated, white trim, grey-blue sliding doors half open, mouth south at y 9), a concrete apron
in 4 m slabs (x -12..12, y -4..9, on into the hangar), a mown grass strip east-west (y -26..-48) with white marker boards, flat fields and low hills beyond a white post-and-rail fence.

## Essential action props (who uses them, which row)
- Cessna spot + yellow nose-wheel taxi line into the hangar (rows 1-9: salted, sawn, bitten); wheel chocks + tow bar (the plane will never fly again).
- The white-cloth table under the LEFT wing (x 3.6, y -.55; wing underside 1.64 m): big plate, side plate, knife, fork, OIL WINE GLASS (amber), big WATER JUG
  + tumbler, cork mat = the kitchen-scale spot, salt spot (rows 10-17). Bentwood chair tucked in on the south side (row 10 "sits, tucks his chair in").
- The owner's rolling tool chest by the nose with polish tin and folded yellow rags (rows 1, 8: he polishes the prop); his deck chair by the hangar mouth
  facing the plane, crate table with thermos, transistor radio, spare rags (row 25: two years, the beard grows).
- Hangar doors: the dessert trolley rolls out of the gap (row 27); the press flashes from the dark doorway (row 32); four gooseneck work lamps snap on at dusk.

## Interactive surfaces
Table top and cloth (plates clink, bites drop), wing underside (shade line M09 travels along it, row 10), concrete in front of the nose (sawdust puff, row 7),
the door gap (trolley path along the taxi line), the deck chair (owner sits, two years).

## Environmental evidence
Two worn wheel ruts across the grass from the apron to the strip (the plane used to fly), tie-down rings with no ropes on the left wing (it is not tied: it is lunch),
the stack of yellow rags (the owner's obsession), drum pump on the oil drum, step ladder by the wing, spare wooden propeller on the hangar back wall (aero-club relic).
