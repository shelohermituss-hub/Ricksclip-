# 10 IDEAS like "The Instinct That Makes You Crash", "Why Drivers Started Filming the Road" and "The Pink Prison Cell Experiment" (2026-11-16)

## What those three have in common
- **A hidden rule of the human mind or body**, shown working on ordinary people in one small place: a straight empty road, a jail cell, a car's windscreen.
- **The "fix" makes it worse, or works for a strange reason:** the flashing arrow doubles the crashes; the pink calms people but only for 15 minutes.
- **An authority figure falls for it himself:** the sheriff hits the stump.
- **Numbers you can see:** a crash every week, exactly 15 minutes, 300 lb.
- **A "you see"** that names the real effect (target fixation, Baker-Miller pink) with an everyday comparison.
- **The twist is a LIMIT or a REVERSAL of the rule,** not just the explanation.

All 10 follow that. None is a made film or an earlier pitch (checked against the project files).

## 1. The Town That Tore Down Its Traffic Lights — 9.5
- **Frame 0:** Workers rip the traffic lights out of the busiest crossing in town while cars stream past.
- **Mockery:** The townsfolk: "Somebody's going to die by Friday." A shopkeeper starts selling bandages at the corner.
- **Escalation:** No lights, no signs, no lines. Bikes, buses and kids all share one open square, and the traffic engineer walks BACKWARDS into it with his eyes shut to prove a point.
- **You see:** When nobody tells you what to do, you look at the other driver's face and slow down. Like walking through a crowded room versus a hallway with arrows.
- **End twist:** The crashes go DOWN. The engineer who mocked the idea is the one who asks for his own town's lights to come out.
- **Fact risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. Drachten, Netherlands, Hans Monderman's "shared space" (about 2003). He really did walk backwards into traffic for reporters. Crash figures vary by source, so no number on screen.

## 2. The Lines That Made Drivers Brake — 9.5
- **Frame 0:** A painter on the deadliest curve of a lakeside highway paints white stripes across the lanes, closer and closer together.
- **Mockery:** A trucker: "Paint won't stop a truck, buddy."
- **Escalation:** Cars fly off that S-curve every month; signs and speed limits change nothing. The stripes go in, and drivers stamp on their brakes.
- **You see:** Lines coming closer together make your eyes think you're speeding up, so your foot slows down without asking you. Like a treadmill that feels faster when the floor pattern rushes by.
- **End twist:** Nobody drove slower on purpose; they all swear they weren't speeding. Crashes on the curve fall by about a third, and the trucker brakes the hardest.
- **Fact risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. Lake Shore Drive, Chicago, 2006: about 36 % fewer crashes reported in the following six months. Tell it as "about a third".

## 3. The Room Filled With Smoke and Nobody Moved — 9.5
- **Frame 0:** A student fills in a form while white smoke pours out of a wall vent, and he keeps writing.
- **Mockery:** None out loud. The two men beside him shrug and keep filling in their forms, calm as cows.
- **Escalation:** The smoke gets so thick he waves it out of his face to see the page. Still nobody stands up. Four minutes, six minutes.
- **You see:** When something odd happens, we check other people's faces first. If they're calm, we decide it must be fine. Like a fire alarm at school that everyone ignores because nobody else moves.
- **End twist:** The two calm men were actors. Alone in the room, almost everyone reported the smoke within minutes; with two calm strangers, only about 1 in 10 did.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. Latané and Darley, 1968, Columbia University.

## 4. The Surgery Where the Surgeon Did Nothing — 9.5
- **Frame 0:** A surgeon cuts three tiny holes in a man's knee, splashes some water around, and sews him back up.
- **Mockery:** A nurse whispers: "Doctor, you forgot the operation."
- **Escalation:** Two years later the man climbs stairs, plays with his grandkids and thanks the surgeon. The patients who got the REAL operation are doing just as well.
- **You see:** Expecting to get better can make you feel better, like a scraped knee that stops hurting the moment a parent kisses it.
- **End twist:** The surgeon had built the whole study to prove his own operation worked, and his fake surgery proved it didn't.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. Moseley et al., New England Journal of Medicine 2002, knee osteoarthritis arthroscopy, Houston.

## 5. Why Fire Trucks Turned Yellow — 9.0
- **Frame 0:** A city paints a brand-new fire truck bright lime-yellow, and the firemen refuse to get in.
- **Mockery:** The chief: "Fire trucks are RED. We look like a lemon."
- **Escalation:** Red trucks keep getting hit at dusk crossings. The yellow ones, side by side for years, almost never do.
- **You see:** At dusk your eyes lose red first: a red truck goes grey and dark, while yellow-green stays bright. Like a red ball that disappears in the evening grass.
- **End twist:** The red trucks had about twice the crashes of the yellow ones, and most cities still paint them red because people expect red.
- **Fact risk:** MEDIUM-LOW. Dallas (from the 1970s), Solomon and King 1995 (Journal of the American Optometric Association). Say "about twice as many".

## 6. Seven Men Who Lied About a Line — 9.0
- **Frame 0:** Seven men in a row all point at the SHORTEST line on a card and say it is the longest.
- **Mockery:** The eighth man (the real subject) laughs, then stops laughing as all seven repeat it with straight faces.
- **Escalation:** Card after card. He sweats, rubs his eyes, and on the twelfth card he points at the short line too.
- **You see:** Standing alone against a group feels like touching a hot stove. Like laughing at a joke you didn't get because everyone else laughs.
- **End twist:** Most people gave in at least once, but one honest friend in the room was enough to make almost everyone tell the truth.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. Asch, 1951-56 (about 75 % gave in at least once; a single ally cut that sharply).

## 7. The Factory Where Darker Meant Faster — 9.0
- **Frame 0:** Engineers turn the factory lights DOWN until the workers can barely see their wires, and the work speeds up.
- **Mockery:** The foreman: "Next you'll have them work with their eyes shut."
- **Escalation:** Brighter lights: faster. Dimmer: faster. Back to normal: faster again. The engineers tear their hair out.
- **You see:** Nothing changed about the lights that mattered. Being watched did. Like cleaning your room twice as fast when your mom stands in the door.
- **End twist:** When the study ended and the engineers left, the work slowed back down.
- **Fact risk:** MEDIUM. Hawthorne Works, Western Electric, 1924-27. The illumination studies are real, and later researchers argue about how big the effect was, so tell it as "the engineers swore".

## 8. The Accordion That Sold the Wine — 9.0
- **Frame 0:** A supermarket manager slips an accordion record onto the shop's speaker, and a woman grabs a bottle of French wine.
- **Mockery:** The wine seller: "Music doesn't buy wine."
- **Escalation:** French music days, French wine flies off the shelf; German beer-hall music days, the German wine sells. Same shelf, same prices.
- **You see:** A sound can quietly open a door in your memory and push your hand toward it. Like walking past a bakery and suddenly wanting toast.
- **End twist:** Asked at the till, almost none of the shoppers said the music had anything to do with it, and most didn't even remember hearing it.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. North, Hargreaves and McKendrick, Nature 1997, a UK supermarket (only 1 in 44 shoppers said the music affected their choice).

## 9. The Fly That Saved an Airport Money — 8.5
- **Frame 0:** A cleaning manager paints a tiny black fly inside every urinal at a busy airport.
- **Mockery:** The cleaners: "Now we have to clean the flies too."
- **Escalation:** Men stop, stare and take aim. The floor stays dry, and the mop buckets sit unused.
- **You see:** Give people a target and they can't help aiming at it. Like throwing a crumpled paper at the bin instead of just dropping it.
- **End twist:** It costs almost nothing, and airports and stadiums around the world copy the fly. Nobody can say exactly how much it saves (claims run to 80 %), but the floors stay drier.
- **Fact risk:** MEDIUM. Schiphol, Amsterdam, 1990s, Jos van Bedaf. The 80 % figure is the airport's claim, not a study. Gentle bathroom humour, kid-safe if staged tastefully.

## 10. The Box That Cured a Missing Arm — 8.5
- **Frame 0:** A doctor cuts holes in a cardboard box, props a mirror inside, and asks a man to put in an arm he doesn't have.
- **Mockery:** The man: "Doc, it's been gone for ten years."
- **Escalation:** The missing hand has been clenched in a painful cramp for ten years. In the mirror, his good hand looks like the missing one. He opens it slowly…
- **You see:** The brain believes what it sees. When it "sees" the lost hand open, it lets the cramp go. Like flinching at a fake spider even when you know it's fake.
- **End twist:** After weeks of practice, his "phantom" arm stopped hurting, and later it faded away for good.
- **Fact risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. V.S. Ramachandran's mirror box, 1990s. Results vary between patients, so say "for many patients".

## Pick
1. **Traffic lights torn down**: the closest kind to Rick's crash tree (a road, a crowd sure it will go wrong, an engineer walking backwards into traffic as a gag, and a reversal at the end).
2. **The smoke room**: the strongest image you can read with the sound off (thick smoke, calm faces) and a twist that changes the whole scene ("they were actors").
3. **Fake knee surgery**: the purest pink-cell kind (a scientist, a strange procedure, a reversal on the man who designed it).
