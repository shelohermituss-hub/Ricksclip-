# 10 IDEAS FOR THE NEXT CLIP, 2026-11-16

Made from all 24 Rick scripts in `references/video`. Checked against every film and pitch in `episodes/`. None of them has been made. Ideas 6, 7 and 9 were pitched once before (memory/onoda-episode.md) and not used. Idea 3 appears as an example in RICK_SCRIPT_PATTERN.

## What Rick's scripts do (the viral score measures these)
1. **Frame 0 is a strange act, in one sentence** ("He loaded the shotgun with gold", "They painted the jail cell pink"). The viewer has to ask why.
2. **Someone mocks it** with one funny line ("[line from a reference film left out]?"). The mockery pays off later.
3. **It escalates.** Each beat gets bigger, there are numbers you can see (82 holes, 47 scratches), and the story clock runs (night, week, summer).
4. **"You see" comes at 60-80 %.** It explains the mechanism with an everyday comparison (blowing on soup, a phone sliding off a table, elevator buttons).
5. **The last line flips the first frame.** The mocker loses, the "victim" was the planner, or the trick turns on the trickster (cashier → undercover agent, burglar → buyer, knight's paper). Many end mid-image, with no moral.
6. **Length:** 55-65 s, about 170-230 words, voice of a kid telling a friend.

Viral score /10 = hook strangeness (3) + mockery payoff (2) + twist that flips frame 0 (3) + one picture you can see without sound (2).

## 1. Why Prisoners Begged for Monopoly — 9.5
- **Frame 0:** A guard hands a prisoner a brand-new Monopoly board, and the prisoner nearly cries.
- **Mockery:** Guards laugh: "The British spend their war rolling dice for fake hotels."
- **Escalation:** Parcels from a "charity" keep coming. One prisoner only ever lands on Free Parking. Another keeps peeling the board apart.
- **You see:** British intelligence and the maker Waddingtons hid silk maps, a compass and real money inside the boards. The dot on Free Parking marked the right board. (Comparison: a pen with a secret note inside the cap.)
- **End twist:** The guard who laughed hears a wall crash at night. Every bed is empty, and only the board is left on the table, with Free Parking cut open.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. Waddingtons and MI9, WWII. Escape numbers are fuzzy, so no number goes on screen.

## 2. The Town That Didn't Exist Caught a Thief — 9.5
- **Frame 0:** A mapmaker draws a town on an empty crossroads where nothing stands.
- **Mockery:** His partner: "You're drawing towns now? Should I pack for the trip?"
- **Escalation:** A rival's map comes out with the same fake town, Agloe. Caught! They sue.
- **You see:** A trap town is like a typo in your homework. If your friend's copy has the same typo, he copied you.
- **End twist:** The rival walks into court with a photo. Someone saw "Agloe" on the map and built a real store there, named "Agloe General Store". The fake town is real, so nothing was copied.
- **Fact risk:** MEDIUM. Agloe, NY, 1930s, General Drafting vs Rand McNally. The real store existed, but the court outcome is told in several versions. Keep it to "they could no longer prove it".

## 3. The City That Paid for Rat Tails — 9.0
- **Frame 0:** A man snips the tail off a live rat and lets it run back into the sewer.
- **Mockery:** The French governor: "One cent a tail, and this city will be clean by summer."
- **Escalation:** Thousands of tails a day, then tens of thousands, and the rats still get worse. Tailless rats run across the streets.
- **You see:** Pay someone per weed and they'll start planting weeds.
- **End twist:** The inspectors find a farm outside town: cages full of rats breeding, kept just for their tails. The city paid for the farm.
- **Fact risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. Hanoi 1902, historian Michael Vann. The rat farms are reported, but the scale is unclear.

## 4. The Ugliest Ship in the Ocean Was Bait — 9.0
- **Frame 0:** A captain orders his crew to make the ship look worse: rust paint, a broken flag, men in rags.
- **Mockery:** The crew: "We're getting dressed up to be robbed?"
- **Escalation:** A submarine surfaces. Half the crew pretends to panic and rows away in a lifeboat, on purpose. The rest lie flat behind fake walls.
- **You see:** Torpedoes were expensive, so a submarine surfaced to sink a weak ship with its cheap deck gun. Like a cat playing with a toy.
- **End twist:** The walls drop and cannons come out. The "weak" ship was a Q-ship.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. WWI British Q-ships, well documented. Picture: the walls falling open is a perfect frame.

## 5. Why Pilots Ate Carrots to See in the Dark — 9.0
- **Frame 0:** A night pilot crunches a whole bag of carrots before take-off.
- **Mockery:** The other squadron: "He's not a pilot, he's a rabbit."
- **Escalation:** Newspapers say he shoots down planes in pitch dark because of carrots. All of Britain starts eating carrots, and the enemy puts carrots on its own pilots' plates.
- **You see:** The real secret was radar in the plane. The carrots were a cover story, like blaming the dog for the cookie you ate.
- **End twist:** Enemy pilots chew carrots by the crate and still see nothing, while the hero hands his carrots to the cook.
- **Fact risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. Pilot John "Cat's Eyes" Cunningham and the propaganda are well documented. That the enemy truly fed its pilots carrots is a legend, so present it as rumour.

## 6. The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower Twice — 8.5
- **Frame 0:** A man in a top hat tells five businessmen Paris will tear down the Eiffel Tower, and sell it for scrap.
- **Mockery:** One buyer: "Next, you'll sell us the river."
- **Escalation:** He takes the money and leaves. A month goes by with no police and no newspaper story.
- **You see:** The buyer was too embarrassed to tell anyone he'd been fooled. Like hiding the bad grade instead of showing your parents.
- **End twist:** The con man comes back to Paris, sets up the same meeting, and sells it again.
- **Fact risk:** MEDIUM. Victor Lustig, 1925. The second sale is mostly told by Lustig himself.

## 7. The Marathon Winner Who Took a Car — 8.5
- **Frame 0:** A runner waves to the crowd from the back seat of a car in the middle of the Olympic marathon.
- **Mockery:** The crowd cheers the car going past; they think he's quitting.
- **Escalation:** He gets out near the finish, crosses the line fresh, and they nearly put the medal on his head.
- **You see:** In the dust and heat, nobody watched every mile. Like skipping a lap in gym class while the teacher counts.
- **End twist:** The real winner stumbles in, kept going by his trainers with brandy and rat poison (strychnine). The "medicine" was more dangerous than the cheating.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. 1904 St. Louis: Fred Lorz and Thomas Hicks.

## 8. They Painted a Warship Like a Zebra — 8.5
- **Frame 0:** A painter covers a grey warship in giant black-and-white zigzags.
- **Mockery:** Sailors: "Now they can see us from France."
- **Escalation:** A submarine lines up the shot and fires. The torpedo misses behind the ship.
- **You see:** The stripes didn't hide the ship. They made it impossible to tell which way it was going or how fast, like trying to catch a spinning zebra-striped ball.
- **End twist:** The sailor who mocked it asks for stripes on his own lifeboat.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. Dazzle camouflage, Norman Wilkinson, WWI. That it really worked is still argued, so say "captains swore by it".

## 9. The Horse That Could Do Math — 8.0
- **Frame 0:** A horse taps its hoof seven times. Three plus four.
- **Mockery:** Professors come to expose it, and the horse answers them too.
- **Escalation:** Strangers ask questions, it still answers. Experts sign a paper: no tricks.
- **You see:** Hans was reading faces. When the asker's head relaxed at the right number, he stopped tapping. Like knowing your mum is mad before she says a word.
- **End twist:** The scientist proves it with a blindfold, then catches himself still nodding when he knows the answer.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. Clever Hans, Oskar Pfungst, 1907.

## 10. The Dead Man Who Fooled an Army — 8.0
- **Frame 0:** Two officers put a dead man in a uniform, with a love letter and a theatre ticket in his pocket, then drop him into the sea.
- **Mockery:** The submarine crew: "Is he our spy, or our luggage?"
- **Escalation:** He washes up in Spain, the briefcase goes to the enemy, and whole armies move to the wrong island.
- **You see:** Boring details make a lie believable. A love letter is more convincing than a medal.
- **End twist:** The "secret plans" were the fake part; the bills and the love letter were what convinced them.
- **Fact risk:** LOW. Operation Mincemeat, 1943. Risk: there's a well-known film, so it's less fresh.

---

## Recommendation
1. **Monopoly** has the best pure-picture twist (empty beds, Free Parking cut open). The mockery pays off cleanly, and the props are cheap: one set, a hero prop and a camp.
2. **Agloe** has the cleverest twist, a reversal in Rick's style ("and the buyer is him"). It needs a court and a crossroads set.
3. **Q-ship** has the strongest action hook and one shot of the walls falling that fits the kit3d look.
