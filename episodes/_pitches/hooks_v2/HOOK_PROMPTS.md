# Hook-frame prompts for IDEAS_v2 ideas 1-5 (concept images, not the film look)

Two style references, added after CB said "we have rick clips frames also":
- rick_ref.jpg: Rick's salting hook, frame_001. Style only: never his characters, set or story.
- style_ref.jpg: our approved lightning-spear final v2, at 10 s.

Model: Fish gpt-image-2 edit, 9:16, 1K, quality low, 310 credits each (1,550 for 5; the balance is 1,868). Quality medium is 1,260 each and does not fit.

STYLE: Vertical 9:16 frame in the style of the reference images (the look only: do not copy their characters, set or objects): flat 2D vector cartoon characters (round white heads, simple dot eyes, thin black outlines, stick-thin limbs, round fists, bold-coloured costumes) standing inside a colourful, detailed, softly lit 3D cartoon world with real depth. Strong saturated colours, a clear warm key light with long shadows. Eye-level or slightly low camera. Faces stay above the lower third. No text, no captions, no logos, no watermark.

1. THE BOX THE STRONGEST MAN COULDN'T LIFT — An 1850s North African town square at golden hour. A huge muscular strongman in a striped robe and turban strains with both round fists on the brass handle of a tiny wooden box on a small stage, veins popping, sweat flying, face red. Beside him a slim French stage magician in a black tailcoat and top hat waves one gloved hand at him, calm and smug. A crowd behind them laughs and points. The box is tiny against the giant man; it is the joke of the frame.

2. WHY HOT MILK FROZE FIRST — A 1960s Tanzanian school kitchen. A small schoolboy in a khaki school uniform shoves a steaming cup of hot milk into an open freezer, steam curling around his face, grinning. Behind him three classmates hold their cups of cooling milk and laugh at him, and a stern teacher with glasses and folded arms shakes his head. Warm afternoon light through a window, cold blue light spilling from the freezer.

3. THE WIDOW WHO DRILLED HER KITCHEN TABLE — An 1816 French chateau kitchen by candlelight and morning window light. A determined widow in a black dress and white bonnet pushes a hand drill through her wooden kitchen table, sawdust flying. Champagne bottles already stand UPSIDE DOWN in a row of holes. Her shocked cellar master in an apron and cap throws his hands up behind her. Copper pots and wine crates around them.

4. WHY SHIP OWNERS WANTED THEIR SHIPS TO SINK — An 1870s English harbour at grey dawn. A fat ship owner in a top hat and fur-collared coat smiles and points as dock workers lower one more giant crate onto an old sailing ship that already sits so low the water laps over its deck. The sailors look terrified, one clutching a lifebuoy. Gulls, wet cobbles, rope coils, a warehouse behind.

5. THE MIRROR THAT FIXED A SLOW ELEVATOR — A 1950s grand hotel lobby with brass and red carpet. Inside an open brass elevator cage, a janitor in grey overalls screws a big mirror onto the wall, while a long queue of impatient guests taps watches and crosses their arms behind him. The hotel owner in a pinstripe suit stares at him, holding a thick engineer's bill for a new elevator.
