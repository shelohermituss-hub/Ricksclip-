# CREATIVE: Why Fire Trucks Turned Yellow (creative-director, 2026-11-20, tier ULTRA)

Proposals only (12d). The writer and the lead take what fits into PICTURE_PLAN.md; CB can strike any line at the one gate.
Sources read: SCRIPT.md v1, BEATS.md, voice/words.json, CAST_PERSONALITY.md, rules/dept/creative-director.md. No last film was named, so "never the last film's palette" is for the lead to check on the look card.
All times are seconds from voice/words.json (word start). Landing word in CAPS.

What is absurdly true here, and what every idea below grows from:
- A man paints the most loved object in town the "wrong" colour and never argues. He just waits for dusk.
- The loudest, brightest, biggest thing on the street is the one thing nobody sees.
- The Chief loses to nothing but the evening. Nobody trips him.

## 2. THE SIGNATURE SHOT (12b): three candidates

### A. THE LAMP ROOM BEHIND THE EYE (PICKED). Row: the TURN, line 10 (40.24-43.44)
- What fills the frame: we are inside a giant eye, behind the pupil. The pupil is a big round window in the upper 60 % of the frame, ringed by the iris seen from the back (fine radial pleats, like a camera shutter made of curtain). Through it: the town crossing at dusk, the red pumper coming toward us, sirens and lights going, and the hero small at the kerb in teal (gag 10). The dome wall around the window is a 1970s scoreboard of thousands of small round lamps in three kinds: red, lemon-green, blue. On "dim" (41.42) the light outside falls and the pupil window WIDENS (true: pupils open at dusk). On "red" (42.68) the red lamps click off in a wave that runs round the dome, and in the same half second the truck in the window goes dark grey. The lemon-green lamps burn on, which sets up line 12 without a word.
- Why nobody else has this picture: an eye entered as a place with its own machinery, where "your eyes lose red first" is something you watch happen to a wall of lamps. It is not a fly through a pupil into somebody's view.
- Enter: line 9 ends on the red truck in the Dallas bay. On "You see" (40.24-40.9, about 27 frames, eased) the camera pushes into its round chrome headlamp; the lamp glass becomes the pupil window seen from inside. The hero object is the door.
- Leave: 43.02-43.94 (about 28 frames) the camera flies forward out through the widened pupil into the dusk air and comes down over the park corner for line 11. No cut on either side.
- Must be built: the dome with instanced lamps (three states: lit, dying, off), the iris ring with an opening animation, the pupil window showing the real crossing set (not a painting), the headlamp-to-pupil match.
- Cost: its own helper (Ultra, proven alone first like a hero object). The crossing, truck and hero are kit reuse.
- Checks: captions baseline at 71.5 % sits on the dark dome below the window, so the window stays clear; the truck and the hero stand on the real crossing floor (grounding); cartoon inside of an eye, no flesh, no veins, no red or pink walls (the dome is plum-indigo), kid-safe.

### B. THE DUSK BLIND. Row: the TURN, line 10
- The hero, in his shop, pulls the cord of the roller blind on "dim". The blind coming down IS the evening: the shop wall hinges away in the same move and the shadow line of the blind travels down the red truck parked at the crossing outside, red above the line, grey below it.
- Enter: a flight from Dallas into the shop window. Leave: the camera follows the shadow line down to the grass for line 11.
- Build: one new set piece (a hinged shop wall, the blind, a travelling shade mask on the truck). Cost: one new set piece.
- Why not picked: it makes the hero the cause of the dusk, which bends "your eyes lose red". Good second choice if the lamp room proves too costly.

### C. ONE ORBIT, ONE EVENING. Row: the CLIMAX, line 12
- The half-sprayed truck of frame 0 stands alone on the crossing. The camera makes one half circle round it while the sun goes down inside the same move. The red half sinks into the dusk, the lemon half comes up like a lantern, the hero stands at the seam.
- Enter and leave: a rise out of the grass of line 11, a drift on to the street for the Milkman.
- Build: kit parts and a time-of-day lerp. Cost: reuse.
- Why not picked: beautiful, but it is a camera move, not a place only this story has. Keep it as the build of power moment P4 (section 6).

Why A: it is the one picture that shows the hidden mechanism (the story's whole reason) instead of telling it, it is built from the hero object on both ends (in through the headlamp, the truck in the window), and it carries its own colour event, which is what this film is about.

**The teal risk, and how the palette handles it**
- Day sky hue is about 220 degrees and light; the teal is about 178 degrees and darker. Keep at least 35 degrees between them and never put his torso against open sky by day: the cream station, the red truck or a white door is behind him.
- Dusk sky is plum (about 265 degrees), so the gap grows. Give him a thin apricot edge light.
- Dallas has the palest sky; he is not in Dallas, so no clash there.
- Nothing teal or turquoise on any set: no shop wall, van, sign, awning or car in that hue. Street-name blades are navy.

**Numbers kept (11c):** ungraded saturation at least 0.36 for the film and 0.30 by day; dusk frames never under mean brightness 0.31 (the dusk is luminous, not dark); one red; faces white by day; natural grass and sky everywhere except inside the eye, which is not a natural place.

## 8. FIVE MORE FRESH IDEAS

1. **RUNNING GAG: the helmet over the eyes.** The Rookie's too-big helmet drops over his eyes in lines 1, 2 and 7. In line 14 it is the CHIEF whose helmet drops over his eyes, and the Rookie, beside the hero, pushes his own up and sees perfectly. Status change in one prop.
2. **A SET THAT TELLS THE JOKE: the letter chart** in the shop (lines 3-4) reads, in shrinking rows, R / ED / GOES / FIRST. Real eye-chart lettering, readable only if you look. Second detail for the same wall: a framed photo of the station's red truck, hung at "reading distance".
3. **TRANSITIONS THROUGH THE HERO OBJECT.** T1 (9 -> 10): into the red truck's headlamp, out inside the eye. T2 (13 -> 14): the Dallas painters cover the lens in red; during the stillness the full-frame red holds; on "That evening" (58.64) the red drains to grey and the camera pulls back from a door panel in the Chief's yard at dusk. One colour change carries the place change and the time change.
4. **SOUND: the red has a sound and loses it.** The siren is bright by day and falls in pitch and goes muffled as the red drains (42.68). The lemon has one clear note on an electric piano every time it appears, and it is the only sound that rings at full brightness at dusk. Bed: a 1970s shuffle with wah guitar. The film's last two sounds: the truck's own bell, once, on the bonk, then the hero's small sip.
