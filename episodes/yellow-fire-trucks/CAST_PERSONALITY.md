# CAST PERSONALITY: Why Fire Trucks Turned Yellow (writer, 2026-11-20; CB_RULES section 5, the 13 points)

Two leads and a reacting cast: the Fireman-Eye-Doctor (hero), the Chief (mocker), three firemen (Old-Timer, Rookie, Sceptic), the Milkman, the Kid with the red ball, the Dallas Clerk, two Painters.
Everyone is unnamed and invented; no likeness of any real person (FACTS row 3). Preset IDs: the character-artist assigns them.
Bodies from the three-size bible (the Kid is the one child body the story needs). SHORT COSTUMES BY DEFAULT: coats end at mid-thigh, no long cloth anywhere.
Era: a small American town and Dallas, 1970s. Period check done by the writer: sideburns and moustaches are right for the decade; hair over the ears; wide shirt collars; fire helmets are the traditional American shape (high ridged crown, long rear brim, a leather front shield with the company number), no modern visor helmet, no face mask; turnout coats are black with SILVER-WHITE stripes.

## THE COLOUR RULES FOR PEOPLE (proposal to the lead for LOOK_BIBLE, 12c)
- HERO SIGNATURE COLOUR: TURQUOISE-TEAL (about `#12A5A0`), worn as a 1970s turtleneck. No other person, sign or prop on a person carries it.
  Why: (1) it sits opposite red on the colour wheel, so he pops in frame 0 in front of the half-red truck; (2) it is far from lemon yellow, so he never melts into his own truck in the last picture; (3) it is far from the firemen's black and navy; (4) blue-green is a colour the eye keeps at dusk, so he stays readable in the two dusk scenes while the Chief goes grey, which is the story; (5) a turquoise turtleneck is pure 1970s.
  Risk for the lead: day sky and dusk air are blue. Keep the sky clearly bluer or more violet than the turtleneck, and no teal on sets near him (no teal shop wall, no teal van). Second choice if the look card fights it: violet `#7A4FC4` (strongest against lemon, but it goes dark at dusk).
- RED belongs to the trucks and the Kid's ball. NO red on any person (no red helmet, braces, tie or cap).
- LEMON / LIME YELLOW belongs to the truck. NO yellow on any person (stripes are silver-white, the Kid's shirt is not yellow, the lemon is a prop).

Contrast comedy for the whole film: one calm man who has already done the reading, among loud men who trust what a fire truck has always looked like. The confident fool (Chief) is loud and wrong; the quiet expert (hero) never argues, he just waits for dusk. The firemen around them each react in their own way and at their own speed.

## THE FIREMAN-EYE-DOCTOR (hero; THIN body, tall, about 40)
Look at first sight: calm, tidy, clever, a small smug half-smile. Turquoise-teal turtleneck, black braces, black bunker trousers into short black rubber boots; black fire helmet pushed a little back. In his shop (lines 3-4): the same turtleneck under a short white doctor's coat worn open, the helmet swapped for a head mirror on a band. Short neat side-parted brown hair, tidy sideburns, clean-shaven.
**Signature detail (fixed, every view): big round wire-rimmed glasses, the kind an eye doctor would wear. (With them, always, the turquoise-teal turtleneck: the film's signature colour.)**
1. Temperament: calm, patient, dryly amused. Nothing surprises him.
2. Confidence vs skill: both high, and they match. He is the only person in the film who knows why.
3. Fears: one more crash at the crossing at dusk before anyone listens.
4. Motivation: that people SEE the truck. He does not care whether they like it.
5. Emotional habits: when shouted at he goes quieter and looks at the thing, not the shouter; when proved right he does not cheer, he takes a sip.
6. Quirks: checks everything by eye at arm's length with one eye shut; pushes his glasses up with one knuckle before he says or does something clever; wipes a paint drop off a lens without hurry.
7. Posture: upright, relaxed, weight on one leg, head tilted a little, chin level.
8. Movement rhythm: slow, even, exact. Never hurried; the spray strokes are long and level like a man painting a wall on a Sunday.
9. Signature gestures (5): (a) the long level sweep with the spray gun, other fist steadying the hose; (b) glasses pushed up with one knuckle; (c) a thing held at arm's length, one eye shut (the toy truck at the lamp, line 4); (d) a slow glance to the lens with the half-smile; (e) the unhurried sip from his mug (last picture).
10. Reaction speed: SLOW and small. One beat late on purpose; a raised brow where others jump.
11. Relationships: to the Chief, polite and unmoved (he lets him finish, then carries on spraying); to the firemen, a friendly colleague who does not explain himself; to the Rookie, a small nod (the first one he wins); to the truck, a patient who has been cured.
12. Status: the station fool in the morning (line 1-2), the one who knows something (3-4), invisible while the facts speak (5-12), the loser on paper when Dallas repaints (13), and the plain winner at dusk (14). He does not change; everybody's opinion of him does.
13. Never speaks. His answer to the Chief's quote is one more stroke of yellow. Last picture: front of frame, calm, mug at his chin, the lemon truck glowing behind him.

## THE CHIEF (the mocker; FAT body, short, about 55)
Look at first sight: round, loud, proud, chest and belly out, chin up. WHITE chief's helmet with a gold front shield, navy uniform shirt with short sleeves and gold collar pins, black tie, navy trousers, black belt with a big brass buckle, polished black shoes. Thick grey walrus moustache, heavy brows, pink cheeks.
**Signature detail (fixed, every view): the big grey walrus moustache that jumps on every strong word, under the white helmet (the only white helmet in the film).**
1. Temperament: loud, proud, warm underneath; a man who loves his truck like a dog.
2. Confidence vs skill: total confidence; a good fire chief, no idea about eyes. He takes "it has always been red" for knowledge.
3. Fears: being laughed at by the next town; a scratch on his truck.
4. Motivation: tradition. A fire truck is red the way grass is green.
5. Emotional habits: anger goes to his feet (stamp) and his moustache; pride goes to his chest and thumbs.
6. Quirks: breathes on the red paint and polishes the spot with his shirt cuff; hooks both thumbs in his belt when pleased; salutes his truck.
7. Posture: leaning back, belly forward, feet wide, head pulled up to look taller than he is.
8. Movement rhythm: big and stamping; a march with high knees; sudden stops with a wobble of the belly.
9. Signature gestures (5): (a) the stamp with both fists down; (b) the stiff arm pointing at the truck, moustache jumping; (c) thumbs in the belt, rocking on his heels, the proud nod (line 13); (d) the breath-and-polish on the red paint; (e) the parade march with the chin up and the eyes half shut with pride (line 14: this is why he does not see the truck).
10. Reaction speed: INSTANT and huge (the opposite of the hero). Zero delay between seeing and exploding.
11. Relationships: to the hero, a boss who cannot believe his eyes, then a winner who thinks the matter is closed; to the firemen, their loud leader, who looks round to check they agree; to the red truck, pure love.
12. Status: the boss in the right (lines 1-2), absent while the facts pile up (3-12), the winner when the paint goes back to red (13), and flat against his own truck by his own march (14). He falls by his own action: nobody trips him, the dusk does. He is never hurt: a bump, the helmet tipped over his eyes, the moustache bent.
13. SPEAKS line 2, the quote "Fire trucks are RED! We look like a giant lemon!" (6.66-9.42 s; the mouth moves only there; "The chief exploded" 5.34-6.24 s is the narrator). Emotion: outrage. On "Fire trucks are RED!" (6.66-7.70) eyes on the hero, head thrust forward, the stamp on "RED". On "We look like a giant lemon!" (8.10-9.42) eyes swing to the truck, arm pointing, head shaking, moustache jump on "lemon". Then his mouth shuts and he stays puffed up.
   Others during the quote: the hero listens with his head tilted, waits for the end, then turns back to the truck; the Old-Timer stops chewing; the Rookie's eyes go from Chief to hero and back; the Sceptic does not move.

## THE THREE FIREMEN (reacting crowd, never clones; all in black helmets, black short turnout coats with silver-white stripes, black boots)
They are the town's opinion. In lines 1-2 they stand behind the Chief; in line 7 they are the "plenty of firemen" who cover their eyes; in line 14 they see the Chief hit the truck. Each reacts in his own way, at his own speed, and their eyes are always on what they really see.

### THE OLD-TIMER (STANDARD body, about 60)
Look at first sight: comfortable, slow, always eating. Helmet low on the brow, grey sideburns, coat open over a white vest, a steel lunch box in one fist.
**Signature detail: the dented steel lunch box, always in his LEFT fist or beside his boot (the lemon in line 2 rolls off it).**
Temperament: easy, seen everything, hard to move. Confidence vs skill: an able fireman who has stopped having opinions before lunch. Fear: his lunch going on the floor. Motivation: a quiet shift.
Posture: weight back on his heels, shoulders round. Rhythm: slow and heavy.
Gestures (3): a sandwich stopped halfway to the mouth; a slow look down at the lemon rolling off his own lunch box, then up at the truck; one slow shake of the head.
Reaction speed: LATE, about a second behind everybody; his reaction arrives when the others have settled.
Relationship and change: stands nearest the Chief and agrees out of habit; in line 14 he is the one who sees it coming and takes a bite instead of calling out.

### THE ROOKIE (THIN body, short, about 19)
Look at first sight: young, eager, nervous; the helmet is one size too big and sits on his ears. Freckles, straw-blond hair sticking out under the brim, coat buttoned to the chin.
**Signature detail: the too-big helmet that drops over his eyes whenever he jumps.**
Temperament: keen, jumpy, wants to do it right. Confidence vs skill: low confidence, more sense than he knows. Fear: the Chief shouting at HIM. Motivation: to be on the right side, whichever that is.
Posture: straight as a pole, heels together, fists at his sides. Rhythm: quick, twitchy, overcorrects.
Gestures (4): the jump with the helmet dropping over his eyes; pushing the helmet back up with both fists; the ping-pong look, Chief to hero to Chief; a small secret thumbs-up toward the hero that he hides when the Chief turns.
Reaction speed: INSTANT, and too big.
Relationship and change: copies the Chief's horror in line 1, but he is the first one who keeps looking at the yellow; by line 14 he stands next to the hero.

### THE SCEPTIC (STANDARD body, tall, about 35)
Look at first sight: cool, unimpressed, arms crossed. Dark moustache and long sideburns, helmet dead level, coat sleeves pushed up.
**Signature detail: arms crossed high on the chest, in every hold; he uncrosses them exactly once.**
Temperament: dry, restrained, hard to impress. Confidence vs skill: sure of himself, solid at the job. Fear: looking silly on a lemon. Motivation: his pride in the uniform.
Posture: leaning back a little, one boot forward. Rhythm: still; one small move per scene.
Gestures (3): one eyebrow up; a slow turn of the head away from the yellow truck with a fist over his eyes (line 7, his own way of "hating" it: the Rookie uses both fists, the Old-Timer pulls his helmet down); the arms dropping open (line 14).
Reaction speed: SLOW and tiny, until line 14, where his is the only big reaction: arms fall, mouth open.
Relationship and change: the Chief's silent second; ends the film looking from the grey truck to the glowing lemon and back.

## THE MILKMAN (the driver; THIN body, short, about 45)
Look at first sight: small, honest, shaken; a man who has driven the same round for twenty years. White short jacket and white trousers, black bow tie, white peaked delivery cap, thin dark moustache. His van is cream-white with a blue stripe (set and props: no teal, no red, no yellow on it).
**Signature detail: one full glass milk bottle held safe in his LEFT fist through everything, never spilled.**
1. Temperament: mild, careful, truthful. 2. Confidence vs skill: a careful driver who is sure he looked. 3. Fear: being called careless. 4. Motivation: to be believed. 5. Emotional habit: shock makes him go stiff, then he explains with his whole arms. 6. Quirk: checks the bottle before he checks himself.
7. Posture: small, shoulders up near his ears after the bump. 8. Rhythm: careful little steps; after the crunch, frozen, then one big burst.
9. Signature gestures (4): (a) the look left, look right at the crossing before he rolls out (he DID look: that is the point); (b) both arms thrown wide, palms up; (c) the bottle held up and checked; (d) the cap pushed back to scratch his head, eyes going up and up the huge truck.
10. Reaction speed: a FREEZE of about half a second, then big.
11. Relationships: to the fire truck, plain disbelief: it is enormous, it has a siren and lights, and he did not see it; he stands in for all "the drivers".
12. Status: an innocent man who looks guilty, and is proved honest by lines 10-12. He is never hurt: a bump, a spinning hubcap, his cap askew.
13. SPEAKS line 6, the quote "I never saw it!" (24.02-25.50 s; "The drivers all said the same thing" 22.28-23.94 s is the narrator). Emotion: honest, helpless bewilderment, not anger. Arms go wide on "I", eyes straight to the lens on "never", a shake of the head on "saw", then his eyes travel up the huge truck behind him on "it". Mouth shut after.
   Others during the quote: the truck's driver (a fireman in the cab, any of the three) leans out and looks down at him, one brow up.

## THE KID WITH THE RED BALL (CHILD body, about 7; line 11)
Look at first sight: small, busy, serious about the game. Bowl haircut, a white T-shirt with two wide navy bands across the chest, blue denim shorts, white sneakers with long socks, a plaster on one knee.
**Signature detail: the plaster on the LEFT knee.**
Temperament: single-minded, cheerful. Confidence vs skill: sure the ball is "right there". Fear: going home without the ball. Motivation: one more throw before dark.
Posture: bent forward, hands on knees when searching. Rhythm: quick runs and sudden stops.
Gestures (4): the two-fisted throw; the stop with the head tilted; parting the grass with both fists a step from the ball; the shrug to the lens with both arms up.
Reaction speed: quick, then a puzzled hold.
Status: knows where the ball went and still cannot see it, with it lying right in front of the viewer as a dark grey lump: the same joke as the Chief in line 14, played small first. Never speaks. Never upset beyond puzzled.

## THE DALLAS CLERK (STANDARD body, about 50; lines 8-9)
Look at first sight: neat, fair, exact; Dallas in one glance. Tan cowboy hat, white short-sleeved shirt with a string (bolo) tie, brown trousers, a pencil behind one ear, chalk dust on his fingers and one cuff.
**Signature detail: the tan cowboy hat, tipped back with one knuckle each time he checks a total.**
Temperament: patient, dry, takes no side. Confidence vs skill: sure of his counting and right to be. Fear: a wrong tally mark. Motivation: the number, whatever it turns out to be.
Posture: upright, a little forward toward the board, chalk at shoulder height. Rhythm: a steady tick, tick, tick; one mark per beat, never faster.
Gestures (4): the chalk stroke with a small nod per mark; the step back with the hat tipped up to look at both boards; reaching for the ladder, then a second ladder section, for the red board (line 9); blowing chalk dust off his fist.
Reaction speed: slow and even; his one big reaction is the long look up the red board.
Relationships and change: the referee between the two colours; he starts level between the boards and ends craning his neck under the red one. Never speaks; never shows which truck he likes.

## THE PAINTERS (two; one THIN and tall, one FAT and short; line 13)
Look at first sight: a working pair who do what the order sheet says. White bib overalls and white paper caps, both speckled with paint from older jobs (grey and white speckles only; the red comes on screen from the gun).
**Signature detail: the THIN one has a paintbrush stuck behind his ear; the FAT one has a rag hanging out of his back pocket.**
Temperament: the thin one brisk and bored, the fat one careful and a little sorry about it. Neither has an opinion on fire trucks; one of them liked the yellow.
Posture and rhythm: the thin one long fast sweeps; the fat one short neat strokes, tongue out.
Gestures (3 between them): the thin one's shrug at the order sheet before he pulls the trigger; the fat one's pat on the last yellow patch before it goes; both stepping aside, guns down, as the Chief walks up to nod.
Reaction speed: none to speak of: they are at work. Relationship: they take the Chief's proud nod as a compliment on the paint job. Never speak.
