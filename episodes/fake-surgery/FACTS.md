## Best real version to tell

In the 1990s at the Houston VA, surgeon Bruce Moseley (physician for the Houston Rockets) tested the knee arthroscopy that surgeons were doing for knee arthritis almost nonstop. A colleague asked: how do you know it's not all placebo? So 180 veterans were randomly given real scraping, a saline flush, or a pretend operation. For the pretend group: three tiny cuts, no instrument in the knee, a recorded arthroscopy video playing, the leg moved, water splashed. Patients had signed a form saying they might get a pretend surgery. After two years, all three groups had improved, and the pretend group was no worse than the real ones on pain. "Jaw-dropping", Moseley said. One pretend-surgery patient, Eutimo Perez Jr., told ABC News his pain had been "worse than 10", was pain-free for over two years, and "I think that if you believe in something, you can get well." In 2004 Medicare stopped paying for the operation for arthritis knee pain, and later trials (Canada 2008, Finland 2013) found similar results. Payoff for the hook: the surgeon did almost nothing, and the patients got better anyway.

## Script must avoid

- "The surgeon did nothing": he did make three incisions and mimed the procedure. Hook may use it as the title, but body line must say "did nothing inside the knee".
- "He expected the surgery to win" as a quote or "to prove it worked".
- Any claim that Rockets players were in the trial, or that the VA stopped using the operation.
- "Use collapsed" or "surgery banned": Medicare partial non-coverage only; use fell in some data, not in VA data.
- "General anaesthesia" or "put to sleep" as certain (sources differ).
- "Proved placebo surgery cures arthritis": all three groups improved; the trial shows the operation added nothing over fake surgery for this condition. Arthroscopy still works for other conditions (Moseley: "not an indictment of arthroscopic surgery").
- Presenting the consent-form wording or the Perez quote as anything but the cited source.
- Showing the sham patient as a Rockets player, or the Moseley face likeness (real person); use a generic surgeon.
- Graphic detail: blood, open knee, scalpel close-ups, instruments in joints, bone saws. Keep OR bloodless: three plasters, green drapes, monitor showing a pretend (generic) joint picture, a bowl of water splash.
- Real brand logos on the tower, VA seal or Rockets logo.
- "Bruce Moseley, 2002 study results: 'jaw-dropping'" quote is from a Hidden Brain interview (later), not from the paper.
